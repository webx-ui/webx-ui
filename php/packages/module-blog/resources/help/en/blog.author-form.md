# about

This is what the site says about a person who writes. A profile has no draft: «Save» changes the
site at once, under all of this author's articles too. The account above the form shows whose
profile it is: it is chosen when the profile is made and cannot be changed.

# howto

## How to open a page for the author

1. Turn on «Page on the site».
2. Fill in the «Address» in every language the page should exist in.
3. Fill in «About» and add a «Photo»: they stand at the top of the page.
4. Press «Save», then «Open the page».

## How to add the author's networks

1. In «Networks» press «Add a network».
2. Choose the «Network» and paste the full link into «Address».
3. Press «Save» and check the links on the author's page.

# fields

## title

It replaces the name of the account everywhere visitors look: on the article's page and at the top
of the author's page. The panel goes on showing the account's name beside it.

## role

Visitors see it under the name on the author's page, and it is not printed under the articles.
Search engines are told it too. Left empty, the line is not there.

## bio

Stands at the top of the page, above the list of articles, and can have formatting and links. With
an empty SEO card it also becomes the description of the page.

## photo

Stands at the top of the author's page and is not shown under the articles. With an empty SEO card
it is also the picture shown when the link is shared.

## socials

Without a page on the site visitors have nowhere to see these links. A row with neither a network
nor an address is dropped on saving.

## social-network

Decides which icon stands beside the link on the author's page. The list is the same as for the
site's own contacts.

## social-url

The full link to the profile on that network, starting with `https://`: anything else is refused.
Visitors follow it from the author's page.

## is-public

Until the author has a published article, the page opens but stays out of search engines and the
site map. Switched off, the page answers «not found», but the address is kept for the author, so
it returns unchanged. Without an «Address» in a language there is no page in it.

## slug

Nothing is made from the name here: without an address in a language, the page does not exist in it
and the name under the articles stays plain text. If you change it, the old address keeps leading
to the new one.
