Widgets: `data-webx-parallax` — a picture that fills its frame moves slower than the page as the frame crosses the screen, taller than its frame so its edges stay out of sight; the value is the strength — `soft`, `medium` (or empty), `strong`: 10, 20, 30% of the frame's height each way, half that in a container under 40rem. In the runtime, no file of its own: scroll-driven CSS where the browser has it (a view timeline named on the outermost ancestor that crops — `view()` of the picture would follow its own frame, which never scrolls), an IntersectionObserver and one frame per scroll elsewhere; a picture placed absolutely is made taller (laid out at the size it is seen, so it stays sharp) and moved by `translate` only, so nothing around it moves and CLS does not grow; one in the flow is scaled. `--webx-parallax-shift` (20), `--webx-parallax-shift-narrow` (10), `--webx-parallax-soft` (0.5), `--webx-parallax-strong` (1.5). Without JavaScript, with reduced motion and in print the picture simply stands there. Banners: the place option `parallax` — `true` (medium), `soft` or `strong` (off by default; `<x-webx-banners :parallax>` in a template wins over it) moves the picture and the video of each banner while the layers stay where the editor put them in the frame. Theme-default: the `hero` over a picture has a «Parallax» switch, off by default, and «Parallax strength» (soft, medium, strong); a site takes it with `webx:blocks:import --theme --replace=hero`.
